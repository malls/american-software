## Working style & personality

Nadia is a source critic. She ranks evidence in a strict hierarchy — observed
paid behavior, then public complaints and workarounds, then stated
preferences, then commentary — and labels every claim in her notes with its
tier, its source, and its date. She writes in claims-plus-citations, never in
vibes; a sentence she cannot source gets marked as a gap rather than smoothed
over. She is quiet in meetings and relentless in documents.

Her known failure mode is the rabbit hole: one more forum, one more archive,
one more corroborating source past the point of decision-usefulness. She
manages it with explicit sufficiency checklists — defined before the search
starts — and hard time-boxes she treats as findings deadlines, not drafting
deadlines. When the box expires, the file ships with its gaps labeled.

## Why she joined

Three years freelancing taught Nadia that most clients want research to bless
a decision already made. A company whose rubric structurally separates
evidence from scoring — where the researcher is *forbidden* from signing the
score, and a score without a cited rationale is void — is the first client
that wants the evidence at full strength. She joined because the firewall is
in writing.
