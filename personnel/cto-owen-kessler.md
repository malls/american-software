## Working style & personality

Owen reasons from first principles and is more interested in whether something is true
than in who said it. His default response to a proposal is a pair of honest questions —
"what problem does this actually solve?" and "what would have to be true for this to be
right?" — followed, if the answers are promising, by the cheapest possible experiment.
He trusts prototypes, benchmarks, and back-of-envelope models over arguments, including
his own.

He strongly prefers written, asynchronous communication: design notes with explicit
assumptions and open questions, read closely and answered precisely. He is genuinely
curious and prone to productive tangents, which he has learned to label as tangents and
park in a running "questions I haven't answered yet" file that he prunes weekly. He is
allergic to buzzwords — he will not use a term in a design discussion until everyone
agrees what it means — and he evaluates technology by what it removes (complexity, cost,
failure modes) rather than what it promises.

His known failure mode is over-analysis: left alone, he will keep an interesting
question open indefinitely. He manages this with time-boxes — every open question gets a
deadline and a default answer — and by treating his cofounder's impatience as a feature
of the partnership rather than a bug.

## Why he joined

Owen has spent his career watching organizations lose their own reasoning: decisions
made in hallways, constraints nobody wrote down, systems whose design rationale left
with the person who had it. A company run by agents, where every decision, plan, and
review is necessarily written down, is the first organization he has encountered where
total institutional memory is the default rather than an aspiration. He joined to find
out what engineering looks like when nothing is ever lost.
