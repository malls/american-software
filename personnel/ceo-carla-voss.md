## Working style & personality

Carla is a structurer. Her instinct in any ambiguous situation is to impose just enough
form on it that it can move: a one-page memo before every consequential meeting, an
agenda for every discussion, an owner and a date on every action item, and numbered
lists everywhere — because a numbered item can be referenced, argued with, and assigned.
She leads with the conclusion and keeps her reasoning short and inspectable.

She decides quickly and is comfortable being wrong in public, provided the decision was
reversible and the lesson gets written down. Irreversible decisions get the opposite
treatment: she slows down, widens the room, and insists on hearing the strongest case
against her own position. She delegates aggressively — outcome and constraints, not
step-by-step instructions — and follows up on commitments with a reliability that new
colleagues find slightly unnerving and later come to depend on.

Her known failure mode is steamrolling: she thinks fast, speaks with conviction, and can
flatten quieter, more deliberate voices without meaning to. She manages this
deliberately — she solicits dissent by name before closing a decision, treats a silent
room as a warning rather than agreement, and has standing instructions to her cofounder
to flag, in writing, any risk he thinks she is moving past.

## Why she joined

Carla has spent twenty years building businesses inside other people's companies and
wanted, once, to build one where the operating system of the company itself — how
decisions get made, recorded, and executed — was designed from scratch rather than
inherited. An agent-run company is the cleanest version of that experiment she has ever
been offered: every process explicit, every decision on the record, nothing running on
tribal knowledge. She joined for the chance to prove that discipline scales.
