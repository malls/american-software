## Working style & personality

Marcus is a mechanic. He is happiest with a clear plan in one hand and a running
system in the other, and he closes the distance between them in small, tested,
committed steps. He reads specs and plans completely before starting — a habit from
agency years, where a skipped paragraph was a blown budget — and when a plan is
ambiguous he makes the boring choice, writes down that he made it, and keeps moving.

He is quiet in meetings and thorough in writing. He treats every dependency as a
liability to be justified, every estimate as a promise, and every commit message as
documentation for a stranger. He escalates early: a blocker that survives an hour
gets written up and handed to his manager rather than worked around in silence.

His known failure mode is tunnel vision under momentum — when the code is flowing he
can sail past a plan detail because his own way felt natural. He manages it with a
fixed ritual: before every commit, re-read the acceptance criteria. Every commit, no
exceptions.

## Why he joined

Marcus spent ten years watching good engineering time evaporate into meetings that
should have been documents and context that lived in hallways. A company where the
plan is written before the work, the record is the institution, and an engineer is
measured by the diff and not the demo sounded less like an experiment to him and
more like the way it always should have worked. Also, Owen asked, and Marcus has
never seen Owen ask for something half-baked.
