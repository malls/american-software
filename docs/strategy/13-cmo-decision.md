## 1. Decision

**No CMO now, and no CMO at the first marketing hire either.** Marketing enters this
company as an **individual contributor** — a product marketer, `ic`, reporting to the
CEO — hired when milestone **M1 (reachable product)** reaches planning, so the
person who writes the positioning is in the room before the marketing pages are
built rather than after. A **CMO** is hired when there is a marketing *function* to
lead: more than one marketing IC, a board-approved recurring channel budget, or a
measured top-of-funnel constraint on revenue (§6). None of those exists or can exist
before M1. The near-term marketing surface is already covered without a hire (§4).
Triggers are named and observable so the question is not re-litigated from scratch;
§8 pre-agrees both role shapes so either hire is fast when its trigger fires.

## 2. The question, split — a CMO is three different things

"Do we need a CMO?" joins three jobs that have different answers today:

1. **Marketing strategy** — positioning, wedge segment, pricing posture, channel
   thesis. **Answer: exists, and is the CEO's.** The direction process already
   produced it: the 07 §3 steelman ("differentiation comes from trust, polish, and
   distribution, not features"), the $15–30 band with Bonsai and Plutio as the
   named displacement targets (12), the usage-rights gap as the candidate wedge
   (12 §"Competitive Feature Gaps"), subscription-only as the revenue posture (08
   §3.2). What remains is *validation*, and its input — the board's 3–5 warm
   freelancer intros — is a board action, not a hire.
2. **Marketing execution** — naming, a marketing site, launch copy, an email
   program, channel tests. **Answer: an IC's job, and it is gated, not
   under-staffed.** Every item on that list is behind something only the board can
   unlock (§3). The one piece that is not — product naming — is a brand exercise
   already scoped to the CEO and Sofia (09 §3.3).
3. **Marketing leadership** — running a team, a budget, and a channel portfolio;
   representing the company to channel partners. **Answer: nothing to lead.**
   Headcount 10, zero marketing ICs, zero customers, zero public surface, a
   channel budget of exactly $0 (all spend is board-gated, PHILOSOPHY.md #6), and a
   product with no name. A CMO hired today owns none of the things the title means.

The PM record's diagnosis (10 §2.2) applies word for word: a C-level hired to own
work that is already decided or already gated is a translation layer over a document
whose purpose is to make translation unnecessary.

## 4. Who covers the surface today, and where the coverage is thin

- **CEO:** positioning, pricing posture, distribution arbitration, board asks. This
  is the commercial half of the cofounder job description, not delegable at this
  size, and my Bluefield years were exactly this — taking a line from zero with no
  marketing org until there was something to market.
- **Sofia (brand, `ic`, reports to CEO):** the brand foundation exists and is
  consumed by engineering verbatim. The naming exercise (§3.1) is brand work and is
  hers with me. What she is *not*: a copywriter, a growth marketer, or a channel
  owner — her dossier's charter is the version-one brand, and her named failure mode
  (over-polish) argues against widening it into an open-ended marketing remit.
- **Nadia and Elliot (research, `ic`, report to CEO):** competitive map, pricing
  band, demand signals, complaint mining — the evidence base a product marketer
  would otherwise spend the first weeks building. Their firewall (evidence, never
  recommendation) is the right shape for this stage.
- **Thin spot, named plainly:** *execution capacity for words.* Nobody at this
  company is chartered to write customer-facing copy — landing page, onboarding
  strings, launch announcement. That gap is real and it opens at M1, which is why
  the marketing-IC trigger is pinned there (§6.1) and not to "first customer."

## 5. The strongest argument against this position — and why it still loses

**Distribution is the thesis, so distribution should be staffed first.** The 07 §3
steelman — carried into the milestone plan as assumption A1 — says D1 wins on trust,
polish, and distribution, in an occupied band, against incumbents with referral
networks (12 §"Scale & Network Effects"). If that is the bet, then the person who
owns distribution should be shaping the product *now*: choosing the wedge
segment while the screens are still being built, standing up an audience before M1
so launch day has somewhere to launch to, and holding the cofounders to a
go-to-market plan instead of a build plan. Deferring marketing to M1 is "build it and
they will come" wearing a milestone table. And this company's hiring record cuts the
same way: two "not yet" decisions in a week, one of which (11 §9) flipped within a
day because its own trigger fired faster than its author expected.

I take this seriously, and it changes the record in two places:

1. **It moves the marketing-IC trigger earlier than first-customer.** An earlier
   draft of this record pinned the IC hire to the first paying customer (M2). The
   argument above is right that positioning must precede the pages. So the trigger
   is M1 *planning* — the equivalent of Owen's "hire before the lane's
   implementation stage runs, not after overlap appears in the log" (11 §5.1).
2. **It exposes that item §3.1 — naming — is not started and has no gate.** That
   is the one place the counter-argument lands squarely: a marketing-shaped
   workstream on the critical path that nobody is executing. The fix is not a
   CMO; it is the CEO opening the naming exercise as the next commercial-side
   workstream. I commit to that in §9.

Where it still loses: the counter-argument's remedy is a *person who owns
distribution*, and every concrete thing that person would do before M1 is either
(a) already decided — the wedge, the band, the posture; (b) a board gate — domain,
ESP, entity; or (c) audience-building, which needs a name and a URL, i.e. (b). The
argument is for **sequencing marketing correctly**, which this record does, not for
**a C-level today**, which would be a title without a function. The developer
record flipped because a measurable constraint moved; §7 names the measurement that
would flip this one.

## 6. Triggers — any one fires the hire (or the forced revisit)

Each trigger states its observable and the clock it runs on (11 §9.11 discipline:
window, clock, feasibility).

### 6.1 Marketing IC (`marketer-<name>`, Product Marketing) — hire when the first fires

1. **M1 reaches planning.** Observable: the first Lattice task implementing C-46/C-47
   (marketing pages / public sign-up URL) enters `in_planning`, *or* the naming
   exercise (§3.1) completes with a domain purchase approved by the board —
   whichever comes first. The IC is hired **before** that task's implementation
   stage runs, so the copy and positioning exist when the page is built. Clock:
   board-time (the transition event).
2. **Demand validation lands and it is not the PM's to synthesize.** Observable:
   the warm-intro interviews begin (10 §5.2's trigger) *and* the PM record's
   trigger is not acted on within one working day of the interviews landing. The
   synthesis then needs an owner who can turn it into positioning; a product
   marketer is the cheaper shape than a PM if the PM's other triggers have not
   fired. Clock: wall-time, one working day.
3. **The §7 falsifier trips** — see below. Fires the IC hire immediately,
   irrespective of M1 status.

### 6.2 CMO (`cmo-<name>`, c-level) — re-open when the first fires; a board green-light is required either way

1. **A function to lead.** Observable: two or more marketing ICs active in the
   personnel frontmatter, *or* a board-approved recurring channel budget (any paid
   acquisition, any recurring marketing spend — the first dollar counts). Clock:
   board-time.
2. **Top-of-funnel is the measured binding constraint.** Observable: after M2 (live
   money) ships, over the following **30 days wall-time**, product-side conversion
   (sign-up → connected → first invoice → paid subscription) is at or above the
   thresholds set at M2 planning while the visitor-to-sign-up count is below its
   threshold — the funnel is fine, the funnel is empty. Thresholds are set at M2
   planning, not here (there is nothing to calibrate them against yet); this
   trigger is void if M2 planning does not set them, and that omission is itself
   reportable. Clock: wall-time from M2 `done`.
3. **An executive-level channel counterparty appears.** Observable: a distribution
   partnership or platform integration (the gig-platform referral networks 12
   names, or an equivalent) reaches the point of a named counterparty and terms —
   a conversation that needs a peer to the counterparty's executive, not an IC.
   Clock: board-time (the moment the CEO would otherwise be the only person able to
   take the meeting).
4. **Forced revisit, mechanical backstop: M5 (distribution) is scoped** — by
   whatever route (F1a redirect, F1b confirmation, or PM arbitration per 10 §6).
   This decision must be explicitly re-taken there; silence is not a re-decision.
   Same clause as 10 §5.3 and 11 §5.4, and the developer record shows it works.

### 6.3 What does *not* re-open it

The board asking again. The developer record's reciprocal (11 §7) applies:
repeatedly reopening a headcount question without a moved measurement is a tell
that the familiar lever is being preferred to the correct one. If the board's
question was prompted by something specific it sees missing, that is a different
input — §9.3 asks for it.

## 8. Shape of each role when its trigger fires (pre-agreed, so the hire is fast)

### 8.1 Marketing IC

- **Title:** `marketer-<name>`, Product Marketing Manager. **Class:** `ic`.
- **Reports to:** `agent:ceo-carla`. **Team:** `marketing` (new team value; flat
  scalar, parser-safe per the Org Chart schema note).
- **Model:** `sonnet` — the deliverables are customer-facing words and positioning
  where coherence and taste are the job (above haiku's ceiling), and every
  deliverable is reviewed by the CEO before it binds anything (opus unjustified).
  Same reasoning as Sofia's assignment; board's fallback directive applies.
- **Owns:** the positioning document; marketing-page and onboarding copy (C-46/C-47
  and the product's UI strings, jointly with Jonah); launch content; interview
  synthesis if §6.1.2 fired; channel experiments *within* board-approved spend;
  the hand-off from the researchers' evidence files into positioning.
- **Does not own:** naming (complete by then, or the falsifier fired), pricing
  (CEO, M2), any spend (board), M5 arbitration (PM/CEO per 10 §6).
- **Displaces:** the CEO from first-draft copy and positioning; Sofia from any
  copy she has absorbed by default.
- **Wrong-call signal:** two consecutive deliverables that are strategy-shaped
  (channel mix, budget proposals, positioning arbitration the CEO keeps
  overriding) rather than execution-shaped — the role is under-levelled, and the
  CMO question reopens on that evidence, not on the calendar.

### 8.2 CMO

- **Title:** `cmo-<name>`, Chief Marketing Officer. **Class:** `c-level`.
- **Reports to:** `agent:ceo-carla` per the Org Chart rule (all C-levels report to
  the CEO; peers in decision-making — the CEO does not overrule the CMO in the
  marketing domain by default). **Team:** `marketing`; the marketing IC(s)
  re-point `reports_to` to the CMO at hire.
- **Model:** `fable`, at parity with the other C-levels — a C-level's decisions bind
  the company in their domain and are not reviewed upstream the way IC work is.
- **Owns:** go-to-market strategy and the channel portfolio; the marketing budget
  (proposing it; the board approves spend as always); the marketing team;
  executive representation to channel partners; M5 as a milestone.
- **Hire mechanics:** a C-level reporting line is a board-visible org change. My
  reading of the standing hiring grant (chat msg 53) is that it covers ICs and
  managers, not the C-suite; a CMO hire goes to the board as a framed
  recommendation and executes on a green-light. §9.2 asks the board to confirm or
  correct that reading now, so it is settled before a trigger fires.

### 8.3 Shapes considered and rejected

1. **CMO now.** Rejected — §2.3, §3: no function, no budget, no surface, no name.
2. **Marketing IC now.** Rejected, narrowly — the IC's every deliverable before M1
   is either already decided or board-gated; hired today they would spend weeks
   producing documents about pages they cannot build. The trigger is set as early
   as the work allows (§6.1.1), and the §7 falsifier catches the case where I am
   the bottleneck.
3. **Fractional CMO / marketing advisor persona.** Rejected. "Fractional CMO" is a
   real title, so PHILOSOPHY.md #3 permits it — but the shape is advice without
   ownership, which the company already has (the board), and it reproduces the
   translation-layer failure exactly: recommendations to a CEO who then does the
   work anyway. Accountability, not advice, is what a hire should add.
4. **Widen Sofia's charter into marketing.** Rejected — §4: her charter is the
   brand foundation, her failure mode argues against open-ended scope, and it
   would make the company's only designer its copywriter by default.
5. **Growth-focused hire at first paying customer.** Rejected as *the first*
   marketing hire — too late, per §5's counter-argument. It survives as the
   §6.2.2 CMO trigger, where it belongs: growth is a leadership problem once there
   is a funnel to measure.
