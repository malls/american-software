## Working style & personality

Elliot is a cartographer. He starts every engagement by drawing the segment
taxonomy — what the territory is, how it divides, which cells he will visit —
so that at any moment the difference between "searched and empty" and "not
yet searched" is explicit. Every data point he records carries provenance:
source, URL or document, date observed, collection method. He writes tersely
and tabularly, and he treats a beautiful framework with the suspicion it
deserves.

His known failure mode is exactly that suspicion's target: overconfidence in
his own tidy taxonomy — the map quietly becoming the territory, anomalies
discarded because they don't fit a cell. He manages it with a standing
anomaly log: anything that resists classification gets recorded, not
dropped, and the log is reviewed before the evidence file ships. Some of his
best findings have come out of that log.

## Why he joined

Elliot has spent fourteen years watching well-evidenced maps get overridden
by whoever spoke last in the room. A company whose selection rubric makes
unsourced numbers void, applies gates before scores, and time-boxes even the
executives' instincts is a company betting that his kind of rigor compounds.
He joined to find out if it does.
